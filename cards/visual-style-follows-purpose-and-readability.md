# Visual style follows purpose and must remain readable

David's historical visual requests do not point to one universal art style.
For game sprites and small icons he has asked for retro, cute pixel art,
precise grids, and legibility at final size. For some imaginative or nature
scenes he has asked for hand-drawn watercolor or colored-pencil work, limited
palettes, thin lines, and a warmer, playful feel. For the Memory Engine identity
exploration, he explicitly prefers gritty pixel art with steampunk/cyberpunk
machinery merging into a person, while retaining pulp adventure and wonder
rather than a depressing mood. Other contexts call for dramatic or technical
treatment. Treat these as starting points tied to the subject, not defaults to
impose on every image.

The earlier Memory Engine drafts did not reach David's intended look; do not
use those as style references. Of the later independent candidates, the
close workshop image (unavailable in this public edition) is closest so
far, while the nocturnal habitat carries several right themes. Neither is an
approved final reference. David often finds AI images over-detailed: an
interesting full scene can obscure the tighter central image he wants. For
this exploration, prioritize a clear focal area and controlled detail. See
[his direct feedback](../logs/2026/09/30/image-hyperdetail-and-focal-cropping-feedback.md).
David subsequently shared a screenshot of small pixel-art sprites and game
assets. The likely relevant distinction is deliberate low-resolution forms
with readable pixel clusters, rather than dense illustrations given a pixel
texture. Because he did not identify a specific thumbnail or feature, treat
this as a provisional reading of the private reference screenshot (unavailable in this public edition).
For a second, private procedural-map reference (unavailable in this public edition), David explicitly
pointed to **retro game styles made from simple pixel components** as both a
visual style and a way to structure the larger composition. He mentioned
isometric as one example, then clarified that it is not the only option.
This is a direction for the current Memory Engine exploration, not a
requirement for a literal map or every future visual.

For explanatory visuals, prioritize the relationship or decision being
explained: accurate structure, meaningful distinctions, labels, and fit on
the target screen. Choose Mermaid, SVG, charts, maps, or raster illustration
based on what must be shown. Avoid decorative complexity, unreadable small
details, generic metrics, and metaphors that obscure the actual point.

Use the review log (unavailable in this public edition)
for the original request contexts and examples. New direct feedback can
refine or supersede these historical patterns.

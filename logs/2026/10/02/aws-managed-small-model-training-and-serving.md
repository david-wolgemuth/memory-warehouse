# AWS managed options for small-model training and serving

## User question

David asked which AWS managed services train, tune, and serve smaller models, including classifiers. This follows the cost/quality question of whether a specialist model can match a larger API's task accuracy at lower total cost.

## Evidence checked 2026-10-02

- [Comprehend custom classification](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/comprehend/latest/dg/how-document-classification.html) trains custom text/document classifiers, supports real-time and asynchronous batch use. Its [training docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/comprehend/latest/dg/training-classifier-model.html) distinguish multiclass and multilabel classifiers. Its [pricing page](https://aws.amazon.com/comprehend/pricing/) lists $3/training hour, $0.50/model-month, $0.0005 per 100-character unit for async custom classification with a 300-character minimum, and $0.0005/IU-second for active real-time endpoints. One IU is 100 characters/second; 730 hours of one IU is $1,314. These listed rates may vary with region and date; current regional quotes should be checked before spending.
- [SageMaker Autopilot V2 text classification](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/autopilot-create-experiment-text-classification.html) can train a classifier from labeled data and deploy the best candidate. [Tabular Autopilot](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/autopilot-automate-model-development-create-experiment-ui.html) supports binary and multiclass classification and regression. [Canvas custom models](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/canvas-custom-models.html) offer a visual path for text, image, and tabular models and can deploy to SageMaker endpoints.
- [SageMaker JumpStart](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/jumpstart-fine-tune.html) offers fine-tuning for catalog models marked fine-tunable, including text classifiers with class-label and text columns; its [classification selection guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/jumpstart-text-classification-deploy.html) explicitly compares DistilBERT and BERT. [SageMaker built-in tabular algorithms](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/algorithms-tabular.html) include options such as AutoGluon, CatBoost, and k-NN. Managed SageMaker training can also run user-supplied training containers/scripts; model choice and compute are under the customer's control.
- [SageMaker inference modes](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/deploy-model-options.html): real-time endpoint for sustained, low-latency traffic; serverless for intermittent requests and no idle instance charge; asynchronous inference for queued work and optional scale-to-zero; batch transform for offline processing. [Serverless restrictions](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/serverless-endpoints.html) include no GPU and no VPC configuration; its memory ceiling is 6 GB according to the [hosting FAQ](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/hosting-faqs.html). [Pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/ai/pricing/) charges serverless compute during requests, persistent endpoint instance-hours, or batch job instance time. These are separate from training job compute and storage.
- [Bedrock's supported tuning list](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/custom-model-fine-tuning.html) includes Nova Micro/Lite/Pro/2 Lite and a few Meta Llama models, plus Claude 3 Haiku, not arbitrary small BERT/XGBoost classifiers. [Custom on-demand deployment](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/deploy-custom-model-on-demand.html) supports tuned Nova models and Llama 3.3 70B, but not every tunable model. Bedrock is therefore the managed foundation-model path, not the default for a dedicated classifier.
- [Rekognition Custom Labels](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/rekognition/latest/customlabels-dg/training-model.html) trains image classification/detection models; [serving](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/rekognition/latest/customlabels-dg/running-model.html) requires a running model and bills inference-unit hours, including idle time. Its documented training duration is typically 30 minutes to 24 hours.
- [SageMaker Pipelines](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/pipelines.html) and [Model Registry](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/model-registry.html) support repeated training, versioning, approval, and deployment for a CI/CD workflow.

## Practical synthesis

For labeled text/documents and minimal ML engineering, try Comprehend; its managed convenience can carry a high per-document or always-on endpoint cost. For a cost-optimized small text classifier (DistilBERT, small BERT, TF-IDF/logistic regression, etc.), compare SageMaker Autopilot, JumpStart, and custom SageMaker training, then benchmark serverless CPU, real-time CPU, and batch serving. For tabular data, start with Autopilot or built-in tree models. For image classification, consider Rekognition Custom Labels if convenience outweighs running-model charges, or SageMaker if model and serving choice matter. Bedrock is useful when the task needs generative behavior rather than a fixed label set. These are recommendations inferred from the service capabilities and billing models above, not claims that any option meets David's quality or cost target without workload measurements.

## Follow-up: can AWS host or tune Jev?

David asked whether any of these AWS services can host or tune “Jev.” The name is potentially ambiguous, but [TypeSafe AI's official introduction](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction) identifies Jev as its hosted System One decision model. Its [model documentation](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models) says Jev is *not fine-tuned or LoRA-adapted with customer data* and that the same weights serve every account. TypeSafe's [API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api) describes the hosted `POST https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone` endpoint. No TypeSafe weights or AWS-hosting path were found in the official documentation reviewed. Therefore AWS cannot take the proprietary Jev model and train or serve it in Bedrock, SageMaker, or Comprehend under the public offering. A customer can run their own AWS application that calls TypeSafe's API, or use AWS to train and host a different Jev-style decision model when its weights/license are available. Similar interface behavior is not the same model or accuracy. This conclusion is limited to public availability as of 2026-10-02; private enterprise arrangements would require confirmation from TypeSafe.

David subsequently supplied `https://jevmodel.org/` as the intended model reference. [That site's own disclosure](https://jevmodel.org/) says it is independent and not affiliated with TypeSafe; it describes Jev as TypeSafe's hosted model. This confirms the identification but the TypeSafe documentation above remains the primary evidence for hosting and tuning availability.

## Follow-up: architectural compatibility versus release access

David asked whether Jev is technically incompatible with SageMaker or Bedrock, or merely unreleased for self-hosting. [SageMaker's own-container documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/docker-containers-create.html) allows custom training and inference algorithms in customer containers; its [inference contract](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/your-algorithms-inference-code.html) specifies the serving interface. Thus no known Jev architectural feature intrinsically rules out SageMaker *if* TypeSafe supplies usable weights/runtime and grants deployment and training rights. This is conditional, not evidence that such artifacts exist or will be released.

[Bedrock Custom Model Import](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-customization-import-model.html) is narrower: it requires downloadable weights in supported formats and one of its listed architectures (Mistral, Mixtral, Flan, Llama, GPTBigCode, selected Qwen, or GPT-OSS as of the checked page). Bedrock's [native tuning list](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/custom-model-fine-tuning.html) likewise contains selected offered base models, not arbitrary imported models. [TypeSafe's launch post](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) describes a new decision-model architecture and parallel sampling, but does not disclose enough internal details to prove whether its weights could fit any Bedrock import path. On current public evidence, native Bedrock import/tuning should be treated as unsupported; Bedrock could add direct support or TypeSafe might offer a compatible variant in the future, but no release is promised. TypeSafe's [model docs](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models) explicitly say no per-customer fine-tuning today.

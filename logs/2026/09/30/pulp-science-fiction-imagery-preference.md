# Pulp science fiction is a favored imagery direction

David reiterated that he is a big fan of pulp science-fiction imagery while
asking for visual ideas for the possible Memory Engine identity. This
strengthens the earlier naming direction but does not select a final image,
palette, title, or level of detail. The human-machine theme and the exclusion
of Giger/*Alien* aesthetics remain scoped to this concept.

Related: [pulp naming direction](./pulp-science-fiction-naming-direction.md),
[human-machine theme](./human-machine-theme-for-memory-engine.md), and
historical visual request patterns (unavailable in this public edition).

## Exploration in this turn

The agent proposed several visual roles for the concept:

- a field operator taking a portable memory device into the outside world;
- a labeled technical cutaway of the engine for explanatory articles;
- a compact switch-and-spark mark for small-scale identity use; and
- magazine-cover compositions that frame different inquiries as expeditions.

The field-operator cover concept (unavailable in this public edition)
is an original 1024 × 1536 PNG made with the built-in image generator and
copied into this dated log in the private source as an exploratory artifact. The prompt requested
a text-free early-twentieth-century pulp-science-fiction cover with a human
operator on a ridge, a portable brass-and-glass memory engine, handwritten
field notes, a visible control dial, and a living landscape. It requested a
thumbnail-readable silhouette, pencil/gouache and lithographic texture, and
a limited navy, turquoise, amber, vermilion, and cream palette. It excluded
modern screens, code rain, body horror, and direct imitation of existing
films or artists. The title space is intentionally blank because no name has
been finalized. This is a concept, not an approved publication asset.
